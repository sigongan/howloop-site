# Howloop picture-learning standard

## Purpose
Help someone with no financial background understand one useful idea and apply it with a free tool. Readers may be busy, unfamiliar with English, older, or new to Australian finance. Never describe readers by intelligence or imply that difficulty is their fault.

## Default learning pattern
1. Ask one real question.
2. Define the unfamiliar word immediately, in ordinary language.
3. Show three numbered scenes with the same objects carried forward.
4. Put each scene's key amount and a short explanation next to the picture.
5. End with one thing to remember and a relevant tool.
6. Keep the full explanation, qualifications and sources available.

All steps must remain visible on first load and without JavaScript. Optional guided playback needs Pause, Back, Next and Show all steps. Do not start it automatically. Respect reduced-motion preferences and pause when the page is hidden or keyboard focus leaves playback.

## Illustration rules
- Draw everyday things: jars, coins, houses, envelopes, wallets and bills.
- A picture must explain a change, not merely decorate the heading.
- Keep the same house, debt document and original money across steps.
- Distinguish money types by labels and shape, as well as colour.
- Do not put essential text into the SVG. Use readable HTML beside it.
- Give illustrative rates and cash flows clear assumptions. A metaphor must not imply that debt disappears or a return is guaranteed.
- Original art is required. Reference a useful layout without copying another site's images, wording, branding or claimed expertise.

## Language
Use everyday verbs and short sentences. Explain 'interest', 'offset', 'super', 'debt' and 'before tax' on first use. Say 'money you owe' instead of leaving 'principal' unexplained. No score, IQ claim or judgement of the reader.

Use 18px body text, flexible image heights, meaningful headings and visible focus. Check 320px phone layouts, all-step reading and guided-mode changes.

## Current examples
- Compound interest: an explicit teaching example of $100 to $110 to $121 at 10% added yearly. The second year's $11 is $10 on the original savings plus $1 on the first year's interest. Picture coins are not a dollar count.
- Full offset: $600,000 loan debt and $40,000 separate cash. $560,000 is the interest calculation amount. Actual debt stays $600,000.
- Salary: salary before tax and employer super serve different purposes. Take-home pay is after tax and other deductions; super goes to a retirement fund. Package wording must be checked against the actual written offer.
