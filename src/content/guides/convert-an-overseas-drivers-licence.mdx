steps:
  - title: 'The state you live in decides the process'
    text: 'NSW, Victoria and Queensland each run their own scheme, with their own deadlines and their own list of which overseas countries count as "recognised". The same licence can be waved through in one state and require two tests in another.'
  - title: 'New South Wales'
    text: 'Citizens, permanent residents and NZ licence holders must transfer within 3 months of arrival; other temporary visa holders get 6 months before they need a temporary NSW licence. A recognised-country licence usually skips the tests; anything else needs the knowledge test and the driving test.'
  - title: 'Victoria'
    text: 'You must convert within 6 months of starting to live in Victoria. VicRoads uses three tiers: Recognised Country (no tests for most), Experienced Driver Recognition (no tests if you are 25 or over), and everyone else, who sits both tests.'
  - title: 'Queensland'
    text: 'You must get a Queensland licence within 3 months of becoming a citizen or getting a resident visa while living in Queensland. Recognised-country licences skip both tests; non-recognised licences need the road rules test passed before you can even book the practical test.'
  - title: 'What you get, and what happens to the old one'
    text: 'Each state issues its own card in the same licence class as your overseas one. In Queensland your authority to drive on the overseas licence is formally withdrawn the moment the new card is issued.'
