Most people assume a dishwasher works like a shower — clean water in, dirty
water out, continuously. It does not. It fills with a few litres, then sprays
that same water round and round for the entire cycle, straining the food out
through a filter on every pass.

That single fact explains the symptom. When the filter blocks, the machine is no
longer washing with dirty-ish water; it is **recirculating a food slurry** and
spraying it onto everything. It takes food off one plate and puts it on another,
which is exactly what people describe when they say the dishes come out worse
than they went in.

## Finding the filter

Open the door, pull the **bottom rack** right out, and look at the floor of the
machine, usually under or just behind the lower spray arm.

You are looking for a **plastic cylinder** sitting in a round well. Almost all of
them twist **anticlockwise about a quarter turn** and then lift straight up. Some
have an arrow moulded into the top showing which way.

Underneath the cylinder there is normally a **flat mesh plate** as well. Lift
that out too — it is the one that actually clogs, and it is the one people miss.

<Callout type="tip" title="Photograph it before you take it apart">
Ten seconds with your phone saves a confusing five minutes at reassembly, because
the coarse filter usually only seats one way round. It is not complicated, but it
is not obvious either.
</Callout>

What you find under there is usually unpleasant: a grey paste of softened food,
grease and label paper. That is normal, and it is the thing that has been sitting
in your "clean" water.

## Cleaning it properly

Hold the parts under a **hot tap** and work an old toothbrush over the mesh
**from both sides**. Grease traps fine particles in the weave, and brushing only
the top surface pushes them further in.

If it is heavily greased, leave the parts in hot water with a squeeze of
washing-up liquid for ten minutes first.

**Hold the mesh up to a light before you refit it.** You should see light through
the whole area. Dark patches are still blocked.

<Callout type="warn" title="Nothing sharp on the mesh">
No knives, no wire brushes, no picking at it with a screwdriver. A torn mesh lets
debris straight through to the pump, and a pump repair is an engineer's visit
rather than a ten-minute job. Bristles only.
</Callout>

## The spray arm, while you are down there

With the filter out, spin the **lower spray arm** with a finger. It should turn
freely with a light push.

Now look at the **jet holes** along it. Blocked holes are extremely common and
the culprits are always the same: fruit seeds, bits of label paper, and
limescale. A blocked hole is a section of your dishwasher that simply is not
being washed.

Push a **cocktail stick or a thin wire** through each blocked hole **from the
outside inward**, then rinse. On most machines the arm lifts off after
unscrewing a central cap by hand, which lets you flush it out properly under the
tap.

Do the same for the **upper arm** if your dishes are dirtier on the top rack than
the bottom.

## Refit and flush

Seat the filter back in and **twist until it locks**. This matters more than it
sounds: a filter that is sitting in place but not locked lets water bypass it
entirely, so debris goes straight to the pump. It should not lift out without
twisting.

Then run the **hottest cycle with the machine completely empty**. No detergent is
needed, though a dishwasher cleaner or a cup of white vinegar on the top rack
helps if it has been a long time. This flushes softened grease out of the pump
and hoses rather than leaving it to redeposit on your next load.

## If it is still bad

Work through these in order, because each rules something out:

1. **Water temperature.** Dishwashers depend on genuinely hot water to emulsify
   grease. If the cycle finishes and the inside is not hot, suspect the heating
   element.
2. **Detergent age.** Tablets lose enzyme activity after about a year, faster in
   a damp cupboard. Try a fresh box before assuming the machine is broken.
3. **Loading.** Anything that shadows another item from the spray does not get
   washed. Bowls facing the centre, nothing overlapping, tall items not fouling
   the arms.
4. **Hard water.** A white film rather than food residue is limescale — that
   needs salt, rinse aid and a descaling cycle, not filter cleaning.

If all four are fine and the filter is clean, the problem is likely the pump, the
heating element or the water inlet — and that is the point where an engineer is
cheaper than guessing.

## The habit that prevents all of this

**Scrape, do not rinse, and clean the filter monthly.**

Scraping keeps the large debris out of the machine entirely. Not rinsing leaves
enough residue for the detergent enzymes to work on — a perfectly pre-rinsed load
genuinely can come out worse. And two minutes with a toothbrush once a month
means you never have this problem at all.
