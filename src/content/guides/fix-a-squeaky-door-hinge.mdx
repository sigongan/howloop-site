The reason spray oil never fixes a squeaky door is that the noise isn't coming
from where you're spraying.

A butt hinge is two leaves with interlocking knuckles and a steel pin running
through them. Every time the door swings, those knuckles rotate against the pin.
When the film of grease between them wears away, metal grinds on metal and the
whole door panel acts as a soundboard. That contact surface is **inside the
knuckles**, sealed away from anything you spray at the outside.

Get to the pin and the squeak stops properly.

## Isolate the hinge first

Most internal doors have two or three hinges and usually only **one** is
squeaking. Doing all of them means three times the mess for no extra benefit.

Swing the door slowly through its full arc and listen. If you can't tell, have
someone else swing it while you put a hand lightly near each hinge in turn — you
can often feel the vibration before you can locate the sound.

The top hinge is the usual suspect on a heavy door, because it carries the most
load.

## Getting the pin out

**Support the door first.** Open it, and slide a wedge, a folded magazine or your
foot underneath so its weight isn't hanging off the hinge you're about to
disturb. This matters more on heavy or external doors.

Then take a nail, a thin punch or a slim screwdriver, hold it against the
**underside** of the pin where it protrudes below the bottom knuckle, and tap
upward with a hammer. Light taps. The pin will start to rise and the head will
lift clear of the top knuckle.

**Only lift it part way** — an inch or two is plenty. You want access to the pin
surface, not the door on the floor.

<Callout type="warn" title="One pin at a time">
Never lift more than one pin at a time. With two out, the door is held by a
single hinge and the remaining screws take the entire weight — which is how
frames get torn and doors get dropped. Finish one hinge completely before you
touch another.
</Callout>

## What to put on it

This is where most fixes fail.

**Use these:**
- **White lithium grease** — the standard answer. Thick, stays put, doesn't
  evaporate.
- **Silicone grease** — good, and won't stain if it runs onto a painted frame.
- **Petroleum jelly** — genuinely fine, already in most bathrooms, and it doesn't
  drip.

**Avoid:**
- **WD-40 and similar spray solvents.** They're designed to displace water and
  free seized parts, not to lubricate long-term. They thin the old grease, work
  brilliantly for about a week, then evaporate and leave the joint drier than
  when you started.
- **Cooking oil.** It goes rancid and gummy.

Wipe the exposed pin clean with a rag first — there's usually black grit on it —
then coat it generously along its length.

## Back in, and work it

Push the pin back down and tap it flush with the hammer. It should sit with its
head resting on the top knuckle, same as before.

Then **swing the door fully open and closed ten or fifteen times.** This isn't
optional. The grease is only on the part of the pin you could reach; working the
door drags it down through the knuckles and spreads it across every contact
surface. The squeak usually disappears within the first three or four swings.

Wipe down the hinge and the door edge with the rag to catch anything that ran.

## If it still squeaks

**It's a different hinge.** By far the most common answer. Go back and isolate
properly.

**It's not the hinge at all.** Two impostors sound almost identical:

- **The door rubbing the frame.** Rub chalk or a soft pencil along the door edge,
  close it, and open it again — the chalk transfers where it's touching.
- **The latch dragging on the strike plate.** Turn the handle fully and swing the
  door; if the noise vanishes, it's the latch, and a little grease on the strike
  plate sorts it.

**The pin is seized.** Penetrating oil, fifteen minutes, try again. If it still
won't move, stop. A whole replacement hinge costs very little and is a far
better outcome than a snapped pin in a knuckle you can't clear.

Done properly, this lasts years — which is the difference between fixing the
squeak and repeatedly silencing it.
