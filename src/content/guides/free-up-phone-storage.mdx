"Storage almost full" always arrives at the worst moment — usually when you're
trying to photograph something that won't wait. And the instinctive response,
scrolling through your photo library deleting one picture at a time, is close to
the least effective thing you can do.

The reason is that the space isn't usually where you think it is. On a typical
full phone, three things are holding tens of gigabytes each, and all three are
invisible unless you go looking.

## Look at the breakdown first

This is the step that changes what you do next.

- **iPhone:** Settings → General → iPhone Storage
- **Android:** Settings → Storage (or Settings → Device care → Storage)

Wait a few seconds for it to calculate. You get a bar broken into categories and
a list of apps ordered by size. Read it before touching anything — the answer is
almost always different from the assumption. People convinced their photos are
the problem routinely find a single game, or a podcast app, sitting at the top.

## The three big wins

### 1. Recently Deleted (usually the biggest single tap)

When you delete a photo, it goes into a recovery album for around 30 days. It is
**not gone**, and it is **not smaller**. If you've been diligently deleting
photos for a month, all of them are still on your phone.

- **iPhone:** Photos → Albums → Recently Deleted → Select → Delete All
- **Android (Google Photos):** Library → Bin → Empty bin

On a phone that has never had this done, this step alone commonly frees more
space than everything else combined.

<Callout type="warn" title="This one is permanent">
Emptying the album really does delete those photos. Scroll through it once
before you tap — occasionally there's something in there you deleted by
accident.
</Callout>

### 2. Offload apps you don't open

Your storage screen shows apps by size and, on iPhone, the date you last used
each one. Look for the combination of *large* and *last used two years ago*.

**Offloading** is the key idea: it removes the app's program files but keeps all
your documents and settings. The icon stays on the home screen, greyed out. Tap
it and it reinstalls, exactly as you left it.

- **iPhone:** tap the app in iPhone Storage → Offload App. Or turn on
  Offload Unused Apps at the top of that screen and the phone does it
  automatically when space runs short.
- **Android:** tap the app → Uninstall, and use "Archive" where offered, which
  does the same thing as offloading.

Games are the classic offender. One large game can exceed an entire photo
library.

### 3. Clear offline downloads

Every streaming app hoards. Check each one individually — none of them will
volunteer this:

- **Netflix / Disney+ / Prime Video:** Downloads section
- **Spotify / Apple Music:** downloaded albums and playlists
- **YouTube:** Downloads
- **Podcast apps:** the worst offenders, because most auto-download every new
  episode and keep them all. Set a limit of two or three episodes and delete
  played ones automatically.

If you're on unlimited data at home, downloads buy you very little and cost you
a great deal of storage.

## Photos, without deleting photos

If your library genuinely is the biggest category, you don't have to choose
between space and memories.

**Turn on optimised storage.** The phone keeps a small version on the device and
the full-resolution original in the cloud, fetching it when you open it.

- **iPhone:** Settings → Photos → Optimise iPhone Storage
- **Android:** Google Photos → Settings → Back up → Manage device storage → Free
  up space

<Callout type="tip" title="Check the backup finished first">
Before enabling this, open the photo app and scroll to the bottom of the library
to confirm the backup is complete. Turning on optimisation mid-backup is the one
way this can go wrong.
</Callout>

**Then find the duplicates.** Both platforms now detect them for you — iPhone
under Photos → Utilities → Duplicates, Google Photos under the Free up space
screen. Burst shots and screenshots are usually where the volume is.

## What you can't clear

The **System** or **Other** category — the operating system plus caches and logs
— has no supported way to be cleared directly, and cleaner apps that claim
otherwise are generally not telling the truth. Restarting the phone clears some
temporary files. Beyond that, only a full backup and restore resets it, which is
a large job for an uncertain gain.

Treat it as a fixed cost. Your usable space is the total minus that.

## Make it not happen again

Three settings, and the phone maintains itself:

1. **Optimised photo storage on.** Your library stops being the constraint.
2. **Offload unused apps on.** The phone reclaims space from things you've
   abandoned, without asking.
3. **A podcast download limit.** Keep three episodes, delete after playing.

Then a two-minute look at the storage screen every few months, before it becomes
urgent — which is the whole trick with most of these problems.
