A breaker doesn't trip out of spite. It trips because something asked for more
current than the circuit is rated for, or because a fault let current take a
path it shouldn't. Flipping it back on is trivial. The useful part is finding
out which of those two things happened — and you can only do that if you reset
it in the right order.

Most people reset first and investigate never. Then it trips again a week later
and they still have no idea why.

## Finding it

Your consumer unit — fuse box, breaker panel, whatever you call it — is usually
in a hallway cupboard, under the stairs, in a garage, or near the front door.
Take a torch; they are rarely in well-lit places.

Open the cover and look along the row. **You are looking for a break in a
pattern.** All the switches point one way except one. That's it.

Two things worth knowing:

- Some breakers trip to a **middle position** rather than fully down. From the
  side it can look almost on. If you're not sure, this is a good reason to do
  the full OFF-then-ON push regardless.
- If a **whole bank** of switches went off together, that's the RCD rather than
  a single breaker. Same reset procedure, but treat it more seriously — an RCD
  trips because current was leaking to earth, which is the dangerous kind of
  fault.

<Callout type="warn" title="Look, don't open">
Resetting a breaker means flipping a switch on the outside of the unit. That is
all. Never remove the panel cover, never poke inside, and if the unit is warm,
smells of burning, or has scorch marks, close the cupboard and call an
electrician.
</Callout>

## Unplug first — this is the whole trick

Here's what happens if you skip this: you flip the switch, the faulty toaster is
still plugged in, and the breaker trips instantly. You try again. Same result.
Now you know only that "something" is wrong, and you've put a fault current
through the circuit three more times.

So before you touch the switch, go round the affected rooms and **unplug
everything**. Kettle, toaster, heater, phone charger, television, the lot.
Switch off any lights on that circuit too.

You have now removed every variable. Whatever happens next is meaningful.

## The reset itself

**Push it fully to OFF first.** All the way down, deliberately. This is the part
that trips people up — a breaker resting in the middle position will not
re-latch, and pushing weakly toward ON just moves it back to the middle. It
feels like the breaker is broken. It isn't.

**Then push it fully to ON.** One firm movement.

It should stay. If it does, power is back and you're ready for the actual
diagnosis.

## Now find the cause

Plug things back in **one at a time**, and switch each one on before moving to
the next. Wait about thirty seconds between them.

This is slow on purpose. Faults often only show up under load — a heater is fine
until its element draws full current, a washing machine is fine until it starts
heating water. Give each appliance a chance to misbehave.

**If the breaker trips again**, the thing you just switched on is your fault.
Leave it unplugged. Depending on what it is, that's either a repair or a
replacement, but either way you now know, which is the entire point of the
exercise.

**If nothing trips it**, the original cause was almost certainly a momentary
overload or a bulb failing at the instant it blew. That's not a fault and
doesn't need fixing.

<Callout type="tip" title="Label the breakers while you're standing there">
You have just learned which switch controls which rooms. That knowledge
evaporates within a day. Write it on the unit with a marker or a label maker,
then photograph it and keep the photo on your phone. Next time you'll spend
thirty seconds on this instead of ten minutes.
</Callout>

## When to stop and call an electrician

- **It trips with nothing plugged in.** The fault is in the fixed wiring or in
  something hard-wired. Stop resetting it.
- **The RCD trips repeatedly.** Earth leakage is the fault type that hurts
  people.
- **Anything is warm, discoloured, or smells of burning.** Leave it off and call
  someone today.
- **A socket, switch or plug is scorched or cracked.**
- **It trips whenever it rains,** which usually means water is getting into an
  outdoor socket or a light fitting.

A breaker doing its job is a sign the safety system works. A breaker that keeps
doing its job is a sign something needs fixing properly — and repeatedly
resetting it is exactly the wrong response.
